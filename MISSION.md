# Mission: Become a strategic programmer in the AI era

## Why
AI now does most of the *tactical* work — writing the function, the test, the boilerplate. The work that's left, and that compounds in value, is *strategic*: deciding what to build, where the boundaries go, which complexity to accept, and how to keep a codebase changeable. These were the skills that used to mark the junior→senior leap over years. In the AI era they're table stakes, and the engineer who has them early directs AI as a force multiplier instead of competing with it. The goal is to build that strategic judgment now, deliberately, rather than waiting a decade to absorb it by osmosis.

## Success looks like
- Look at a module/function in a real codebase and judge whether its design *reduces* or *adds* net complexity — and articulate why in the vocabulary of design (depth, coupling, information hiding).
- Take a vague feature request and produce a sequenced, de-risked plan that names the irreversible decisions and defers the reversible ones.
- Make and defend a "what NOT to build" / "take this debt on purpose" call with explicit reasoning about tradeoffs.
- Direct an AI agent the way a senior directs a junior: scope the work, define the interface, review the output against design principles — not just accept what it generates.

## Constraints
- Early-career (0–3 yrs): start concrete, avoid "architecture astronaut" abstraction. Every idea must cash out against real code.
- Works on a small team / startup context: wide influence, fast-moving, decisions have real consequences soon.
- Learns best **project-driven** (tie lessons to real work in progress) and **reading-heavy** (point to the best primary sources; lessons reinforce the reading).
- Short, frequent lessons preferred — one tangible win each.

## Out of scope (for now)
- People-management / EM-track leadership (this is about *technical* strategy as an IC).
- Distributed-systems / large-scale infra design (premature for a small-team context).
- Tactical coding drills (AI does this; not where the leverage is).
