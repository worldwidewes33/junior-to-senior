Design Heuristics Card

The compressed essence of the lessons, for quick reference. Grows as we go. Source vocabulary: Ousterhout, A Philosophy of Software Design; Parnas (1972).

The one question

"Is this change making the codebase easier or harder to change next month?"

That's all of design, compressed. Complexity = anything that makes the system hard to understand or modify. Your job is to keep it from accumulating.

Glossary (canonical — used in every lesson)

Complexity
Anything about structure that makes software hard to understand or change. Not "lines" or "hard math."
Dependencies & Obscurity
The two root causes of complexity. A dependency = can't understand/change one piece alone. Obscurity = important info isn't obvious.
Interface vs. Implementation
Interface = what a caller must know (the cost). Implementation = the machinery (the benefit, when hidden).
Deep module
Good. Small interface, large functionality hidden behind it. High benefit-to-cost. open/read/write/close.
Shallow module
Bad. Interface nearly as complex as implementation. Can be worse than no abstraction. A one-line pass-through wrapper.
Classitis
The disease of too many small classes/wrappers. Each adds interface to learn. Tidy-looking, complexity going up. Common in AI-generated "refactors."
Information hiding (Parnas, 1972)
A module is defined around a design decision it hides. Hide the thing most likely to change. The mechanism that makes modules deep.

The depth test

SymptomRead it as
Interface ≈ as complex as the bodyShallow — inline it or deepen it
Caller must know internal details to use itLeaky — info not hidden
Tiny interface, lots hidden, used widelyDeep — keep & protect it
One change forces edits in many filesChange amplification — wrong boundaries

Complexity symptoms (spot these in review)

Change amplification
Simple change → edits in many places.
Cognitive load
Must hold too much in your head to change it safely.
Unknown unknowns
Not obvious what to change or what will break. The most dangerous one.

Reviewing AI output (the strategic move)

"Did each new abstraction earn its interface?"

AI optimizes for code that works and looks tidy, not for net complexity. Watch for: pass-through wrappers, premature splitting into many small classes, leaked internals. Reject depth-negative changes the way you'd redirect a junior.
